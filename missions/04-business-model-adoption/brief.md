# Mission 4: Business Model & Adoption
URL: https://acthouse.notion.site/Mission-4-Business-Model-Adoption-d8e4a99790e082b0b80201606f3096d8

Mission Objective: Build the Path to Adoption and Sustainability
Show how your product creates and delivers value, how a customer reaches that value, and what may sustain it. Keep the original Business Model or Lean Canvas and monetization work, but use it to decide what must be coded before a customer can use the MVP. Separate the person using the product from anyone who might pay for, sponsor, or operate it. Treat untested prices, partnerships, and demand as hypotheses.
Due: October 3, 2026 by 11:59 p.m. Eastern Time.
Mission points: 150. Points are added to the leaderboard after staff review and approval.
Submit this mission: ATL Cup 2026 submission page. Upload one completed packet file with your team name and mission title.

Quote: "A business model becomes useful when it changes what the team builds next."

Deliverable: Business Model & Adoption Decision
Submit one packet file containing:
- A concise Business Model or Lean Canvas: customer segments, value proposition, channels, relationships or support, key resources, activities, partners, costs, and revenue or stewardship. Mark uncertain entries as hypotheses.
- Who uses the product, who may pay or support it, and why each would participate; these can be different people or organizations.
- Up to three prioritized revenue or sustainability hypotheses and a safe way to test the most important one. Do not invent prices, willingness-to-pay results, or partner commitments.
- One coded access or value-delivery step changed or added in the product, with a working link or truthful build-state note and a test result.
- A Mission 5 scope decision: what must work in the coded, digital, AI-enabled MVP; what can remain human-supported at the edges; and what is deliberately out of scope.

Action Items:
1. Complete and compare the model. Start with the customer task and evidence from Mission 3. Fill the canvas briefly, marking observed/sourced, inferred, or unknown. Map users, payers or supporters, and operators separately. Ask how people discover the product, begin the task, receive the result, get help, and regain trust if it fails. Compare plausible models—user payment, organization payment, partner support, or another fitting route—and select one primary hypothesis and at most two alternatives. Consider access, affordability, customer incentives, operating costs, and the required in-product AI's inputs, permissions, cost, failure behavior, and human control. You do not need to process real payments or claim a partner to complete this mission.
2. Implement and test the adoption path. Pick the one entry, onboarding, request, handoff, result, or support step that your model depends on. Define the customer action, system response, and recovery path; implement or improve that step in the editable code project and test it with permitted data. After the team's first canvas and build choice, use FACTS with an approved AI tool: Feed the actual canvas and constraints; Assess proposed scenarios; Challenge unsupported numbers or assumptions; Test the Imagination with a simpler or more equitable route; Steward one checked build or research decision. AI can help code, but the team verifies the behavior. Where permitted, observe whether a target customer can find, enter, or understand the value of the product; do not mistake enthusiasm for payment commitment.

Pro Tips:
- Value to the user, payment, and operation are different jobs; name each clearly.
- Preserve customer access. A revenue hypothesis should not quietly exclude the person you intend to serve.
- Do not add financial-data collection or a fake partnership for a demo.
- A credible route to first use matters more today than an impressive but unsupported market estimate.
- Use the canvas to cut scope for Mission 5, not to create more presentation work.

Example: A community organization may use a product while a local institution could eventually sponsor access. The team labels sponsorship and pricing as untested hypotheses, builds the onboarding and request path the model requires, and asks an appropriate potential user to try that path. It does not claim a signed partner or count interest as revenue.
