# Mission 6: Brand Identity & Product Experience (due Oct 5, 175)
URL: https://acthouse.notion.site/Mission-6-Brand-Identity-Product-Experience-d9c4a99790e0826a80c101bffee993c0

Mission Objective: Make the Product Clear, Trustworthy, and Memorable
Give the working product a recognizable identity inside the experience customers actually use, then begin the pitch deck that tells the same truthful story. Keep the original brand identity, visual direction, and short story work, but let customer friction determine which part of the product to improve first. The "cool factor" is a useful product moment that helps a person understand, act, or see a meaningful result—not decoration over a broken task.

Quote: "Cool is not decoration. Cool is when the product works—and people want to use it again."

Deliverable: Product Experience Revision & Deck Draft
- A concise brand identity: intended feeling, values, voice, a simple logo or wordmark direction, color and type choices, and a two- or three-sentence brand story. Explain how these choices support the customer task.
- One or more implemented changes to the coded product's entry, instructions, core interaction, AI result and explanation, error/help, or return path, with a link to the released version.
- A before/after account of the customer friction, what changed, and what a permitted customer or first-time tester could do in the revised version. Keep actual customer evidence separate from internal review.
- A quick readability, control, accessibility, and trust check, including honest claims and a clear way to recover from a poor AI result.
- A first draft of the required 8–16-slide pitch deck, using current product visuals and the same brand direction. Mark gaps or untested claims for Mission 7.

Action Items (summary of two items, verbatim text in the Notion page): define the brand through the customer experience (pick the most consequential moment of hesitation from Mission 5 actual use; identity statement; voice, wordmark, colors, type, story; then FACTS with an approved AI tool on two interaction improvements; no AI accessibility claim as a substitute for human checking). Implement the improvement and start the deck (change code, retest the core task and in-product AI, release; first-time user check; 8-16-slide deck with current screenshots; show the minimum 10 outreach contacts separately from responses and attempts; mark projections, generated personas and simulations as hypotheses).

Example: Customers hesitate because they cannot tell whether the AI result is final. The team labels it "AI draft," explains it plainly, and adds visible edit and approve controls using the chosen brand voice and visual system. ... without claiming a universal improvement from one attempt.

Resources: Optimizely, UserTesting, Hotjar, Figma, Google Analytics, The A/B Testing Manual, Mixpanel, FACTS ("optional references, not required accounts or permission to collect customer data").
