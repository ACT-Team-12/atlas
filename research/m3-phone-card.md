# Interview phone card (10 minutes)

Use with anyone who helps people after a clinic visit (CHW, navigator, social worker, nurse, MA), or a patient or caregiver who had to follow up. Ask about **the last real time**, never "would you". Don't mention the app until question 7.

**Open (say this):** "Hey, I'm on a student team in the Atlanta Innovation Cup. We're learning how people follow through after a doctor visit, like getting a lab done or making it to a referral. Can I ask you a few questions about the last time that happened? I won't use your name unless you say it's okay, and please don't tell me anyone's health details."

**Ask:**
1. "Tell me about the last time you (or someone you helped) left a visit with things to do after. What were they?"
2. "Which of those didn't happen, or almost didn't? What got in the way that time?"
3. "What did you do about it? Who did you call, what did you look up?" (listen for 211, Google, a spreadsheet, memory, a coworker)
4. "How long did it take to sort that out?" (get a number if they have one)
5. "What's the most annoying part of that whole thing?"
6. "If a tool read the visit paper, what would it have to do for you to trust it? What would make you stop using it?"
7. Now show the site (atlas-team12.vercel.app, "Use the sample paper"). "Walk me through what you'd do." Stay quiet and watch. Note where they pause or get confused.

**Close (the commitment ask):** "Would you try it with the next person you help this week? Can I check back with you?" Write down exactly what they said. A yes, a no and a maybe all count as evidence.

**Before hanging up:** "Can we quote you without your name, like 'a community health worker in DeKalb'?" Note yes or no.

## Right after the call (dictate this, 2 minutes)
- Date and time, role, how you know them (no name), county or area if they said
- Okay to quote? yes / no
- Their answers to 1 to 6, as close to their words as you can
- What they did on the site, and where they got stuck
- Commitment answer
- One thing they'd want changed
