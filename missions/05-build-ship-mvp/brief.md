# Mission 5: Build & Ship a Code-Based MVP (due Oct 4, 175)
URL: https://acthouse.notion.site/Mission-5-Build-Ship-a-Code-Based-MVP-8414a99790e082b2b8ad01a5273741f1

Mission Objective: Build, Ship, and Test the Coded MVP
Build and deploy the smallest coded, digital product that a target customer can actually use to attempt one meaningful task. AI must perform a real function inside that product; AI assistance while building is welcome but does not replace it. Reach out to at least 10 target customers about the MVP, observe actual use, fix the most important breakdown, and release the improvement. There are no no-code or non-digital exceptions for Mission 5. A wireframe or clickable mockup is supporting work, not the MVP.

Quote: "The MVP is the moment your idea has to work for someone besides your team."

Deliverable: Working MVP & Customer-Use Record
- A deployed link or safe access route to a working, coded digital MVP and a route to its editable code/project for review. Never share credentials or secrets.
- A concise walkthrough of one core customer task: entry → action → meaningful result → failure or help path. Identify the specific in-product AI function, its input, output, customer control, and limits.
- A record that the team reached out to at least 10 target customers about this MVP. Report contacted, responded, invited to try, and actually attempted separately; outreach is not product use.
- Evidence of at least one appropriate target customer's attempt to use the MVP, what worked or failed, and the most important product change made in response. If actual use was not achieved, report the gap honestly; the outreach and working-product requirements still stand.
- What is live, human-supported, simulated, untested, or broken, plus a short FACTS decision trace.

Action Items:
1. Build and deploy the smallest complete task. Using the Mission 3 evidence and Mission 4 scope choice, write the team's own brief: "For [TARGET CUSTOMER], when [SITUATION], the product lets them [ACTION] so they can [RESULT]." Specify the entry, AI function, customer review or control, and likely failure state. Cut unrelated features. Build in editable code, deploy a customer-accessible version, and test it from the customer's entry point on a relevant device. AI may generate or debug code, interface copy, or tests, but your team must inspect, run, test, debug, and be able to change the result. Apply FACTS to the real build: Feed the task and constraints; Assess by running it; Challenge failures and unsupported claims; Test the Imagination with an edge case or better interaction; Steward the checked release. A static screen, no-code workflow, or fake AI response does not meet this mission.
2. Reach 10 customers, observe use, and ship a fix. Contact at least 10 target customers about the deployed MVP. Use a permitted contact route and minimize personal data in the record. Track how many were contacted, replied, accepted an invitation, and actually attempted the core task. Give an appropriate target customer a task without coaching every click; observe whether they reach the promised result, where they pause, what they expected, and what the AI function actually did. Do not present outreach as use or praise as proof of usability. Fix the issue that most blocks task completion, test the repaired path, and deploy the new version. Record what changed and what still needs validation.

Pro Tips: Cut scope until one real customer task works end to end. Test the deployed route, not just the editor preview. AI-generated code counts when the team can inspect, run, debug, and change it; an AI-generated design alone does not. Ten contacts are a minimum outreach requirement, not ten users or ten successful task attempts. Label any simulation or human-supported edge honestly. The core coded, AI-enabled task must still work. Keep the result understandable, the controls usable, and recovery clear; a memorable product moment should help the customer, not hide friction.

Example: A customer enters a business question. The product's AI categorizes it and drafts a next action; the customer reviews or edits the recommendation before saving. The team deploys that path, contacts at least 10 target customers, observes a permitted customer attempt, sees that the result is confusing, adds an explanation and edit control, and releases the repaired version. This is an illustration, not evidence that such customers exist or responded.
