# Published evidence for Mission 3 (verified 2026-10-02)

Method: deep-research run (5 search angles, 24 sources fetched, 119 claims extracted, top 25 checked by 3 independent verifiers each; 22 confirmed, 3 refuted). Quotes below are what the verifiers found on the source page. Before a number goes in the packet we re-open the source and check the quote ourselves.

**Use rules:** the comprehension studies are emergency-department discharge, not clinic after-visit summaries (say so). Referral studies are 2007 to 2018 and not from Georgia (say so). Refuted claims at the bottom must not be used.

## 1. Patients retain and understand only part of their discharge instructions, and they mostly do not know when they have misunderstood. Recall averaged 47% for verbal-only (n=1,460), 58% for written (n=3,395) and 67% for video (n=459) in an ED meta-analysis. In a 140-patient ED study, 78% had deficient comprehension in at least one domain and 51% in two or more. Post-visit care was the largest category of deficits (34%, vs 15% for diagnosis), and patients perceived difficulty only 20% of the time when their comprehension was deficient. Design implication: the helper or tool must check understanding actively (teach-back) and not wait for the patient to say they are confused.

- Confidence: high · votes: 3-0 (claims 10-14)
- Sources: https://www.em-consulte.com/article/1350372/patient-discharge-instructions-in-the-emergency-de · https://www.cbssm.org/publication_pdfs/1006/
- Evidence: Hoek et al. Ann Emerg Med (systematic review, 51 studies, search to Mar 2018): '47% for patients who received verbal information (95% CI 32.2% to 61.7%), 58% ... written ..., and 67% ... video'. The authors note the written and video results were not significantly higher overall. Engel et al. Ann Emerg Med 2009: 'Seventy-eight percent of patients demonstrated deficient comprehension ... in at least 1 domain; 51% of patients, in 2 or more domains.' 'Greater than a third of these deficiencies (34%) involved patients' understanding of post-ED care, whereas only 15% were for diagnosis and cause.' 'Patients perceived difficulty with comprehension only 20% of the time when they demonstrated deficient comprehension.' All votes 3-0.

## 2. Many referrals are never completed, and the measured gap ranges widely by setting: 34.8% documented completion in one large health system (103,737 scheduling attempts), 22.9% non-completion within 2 months for pediatric referrals from community health centers (n=341), and about 17-21% non-completion within 3 months in a national primary-care cohort (n=776). Medicaid patients were less likely to complete referrals, and having physician or staff schedule the specialty appointment predicted completion.

- Confidence: high · votes: 3-0 (claims 3, 4, 7, 8)
- Sources: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11606-018-4392-z · https://exa.ai/library/publication/hftjgdd9mx3 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1934973/
- Evidence: Patel et al. JGIM 2018: 'Of the 103,737 analyzed referral scheduling attempts, only 36,072 (34.8%) resulted in documented complete appointments.' Documented undercounts care completed outside the system. Zuckerman et al. J Pediatr 2012: 'A total of 22.9% of families experienced incomplete referral.' Forrest et al. Ann Fam Med 2007 (ASPN): 'Physicians reported that 79.2% of patients referred had a specialist visit, and 83.0% of patients indicated they completed the referral'. 'Patients in Medicaid plans were less likely than others to complete the referral ... physician/staff scheduling of the specialty appointment were both positive predictors of referral completion.' Supports ATLAS doing the scheduling step with the patient, not just handing over a list.

## 3. Transportation, work and childcare are the most common barriers, but in one study the barriers that statistically predicted a missed referral were health-system logistics: getting an appointment quickly, finding the office, and office hours. In the national cohort, the top patient-stated reasons for non-completion were believing the problem had resolved (47.5%) and lack of time (37.3%). ATLAS should treat logistics and the patient's sense of why the step matters as core barriers, not only transport and cost.

- Confidence: high · votes: 3-0 (claims 5, 6, 9)
- Sources: https://exa.ai/library/publication/hftjgdd9mx3 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1934973/
- Evidence: Zuckerman 2012: '42.0% of families encountered 1 or more barriers. The most frequent barriers were difficulty leaving work, obtaining childcare, and obtaining transportation.' 'On multivariate analysis, difficulty getting appointments quickly, difficulty finding doctors' offices, and inconvenient office hours were associated with incomplete referral ... improving health system factors may be the best way to reduce incomplete referrals.' Forrest 2007 (132 non-completers): 'the patient's belief that the health problem had resolved (47.5%), (2) lack of time (37.3%)'. Do not cite either study as evidence that transport is the main cause of missed referrals.

## 4. Front-line staff and Atlanta residents name the same barrier set ATLAS targets. Primary care referrers (UT Health San Antonio focus groups) cite cost, communication, transportation and paperwork, plus constantly changing community resources. Grady's 2022 CHNA focus groups and key informants (Fulton/DeKalb) cite cost of care and insurance, lack of transportation, lack of Medicaid coverage, long waits, distrust, and health literacy.

- Confidence: high · votes: 3-0 (claims 1, 2, 19)
- Sources: https://doi.org/10.1177/21501319251413968 · https://www.gradyhealth.org/wp-content/uploads/2022-Community-Health-Needs-Assessment.pdf
- Evidence: Rodriguez et al. J Prim Care Community Health 2026, abstract themes: '(3) Cost, Communication, Transportation, and Paperwork Limit Patient Access to Services' and '(2) Constant Fluctuation of Community Resources Limit Access'. Only the abstract was verified, so participant quotes and n are NOT VERIFIED. Grady 2022 CHNA: 'Access to care: High cost of care and insurance, inequities impacting specific communities, lack of trust in providers, long wait times, lack of transportation, and lack of Medicaid coverage.' 'Key informants discussed health literacy as a major barrier to residents seeking care.' Both sources are qualitative and give no prevalence numbers.

## 5. Closed-loop referral platforms are underused, and their core feature (the e-referral) is the least-used feature. In Trenton, NJ, four years into a free, subsidized NowPow rollout, only 7% of trained users in 25% of trained organizations used it, and none of four engagement strategies noticeably increased use. Monthly activity averaged 246 shares and 241.5 searches against 8 e-referrals and 2.3 screenings. Users wanted a trustworthy directory to search and share, not referral workflow.

- Confidence: medium · votes: 3-0 (claims 20, 21)
- Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10796276/
- Evidence: Fichtenberg et al. Health Serv Res 2024: 'only 7% of trained users in 25% of trained organizations used the platform.' 'None of the tested strategies (data insight reports, a referral hub, tailored training, and a communication campaign) noticeably increased platform use.' 'sharing information about an organization with a client (... on average 246 times per month ...) and searches (241.5 per month). By contrast, on average only 8 electronic referrals were sent per month and only 2.3 screenings'. Medium because this is one small city (pop. about 84k), data from 2019-2023, users were mainly CBOs, and the 513-trained figure is cumulative (about 10% active in a given month).

## 6. Patients rate EHR-integrated, findhelp-linked referral help much lower than staff do. In Essentia Health's 'Resourceful' system, 56% of patients agreed it provided 'helpful help' reducing social care barriers, compared with 93% of healthcare workers, a 37-point gap and the largest in the study. Incumbent tools look successful from the staff side and less so from the patient side.

- Confidence: medium · votes: 3-0 (claim 0)
- Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12052525/
- Evidence: Health Serv Res 2025: 'provided helpful help reducing social care barriers (patients:56%, HCWs:93%)'. 'Percentage differences ... ranged from 2% to 37%, with ensuring helpful help representing the largest difference.' Small and self-selected sample: 62 of 780 patients (8% response rate) and 14 of 38 HCWs. Setting is MN/ND/WI. A related claim that helpers rated higher on all differing measures was refuted (1-2).

## 7. Georgia has no state CHW certification. DPH describes certification as an advocacy goal, and the certification bill HB 291 died in 2026. The state's CHW infrastructure is a CHW Advisory Board (May 2017), a CHW Advocacy Coalition (April 2018, about 70 members, led by Georgia Watch), and a Georgia CHW Network (launched 2021).

- Confidence: high · votes: 3-0 (claims 15, 16)
- Sources: https://dph.georgia.gov/chronic-disease-prevention/community-health-workers
- Evidence: Georgia DPH page (last reviewed 4/16/26): the coalition 'is advocating for formal recognition of CHWs as certified health professionals in Georgia'. The page also gives the 2017, 2018 and 2021 establishment dates and 'approximately 70 members'. The verifier confirmed via BillTrack50 that GA HB 291 (CHW certification) is 'Dead as of 04/02/2026'. The infrastructure list is a paraphrase of the page, not a verbatim quote. Voluntary non-state certificates were not checked. Implication: ATLAS cannot assume a credentialed, uniformly trained Georgia CHW user, and a bounded, guided workflow is a better fit.

## 8. Atlanta's core counties have language and transport gaps relevant to completing after-visit steps. 8.3% of DeKalb residents have limited English proficiency (Fulton 5.0%, Georgia 5.4%). 8.6% of DeKalb and 10.8% of Fulton households have no vehicle (Georgia 6.7%), and focus groups described MARTA as a strength for those living close to a station or bus stop.

- Confidence: medium · votes: 3-0 (claims 17, 18)
- Sources: https://www.gradyhealth.org/wp-content/uploads/2022-Community-Health-Needs-Assessment.pdf
- Evidence: Grady 2022 CHNA, ACS 2016-2020: 'DeKalb County in particular has a higher percentage of limited English-speaking skills (8.3%) compared to both Fulton County and the state (5.0% and 5.4%).' '8.6% of households in DeKalb County and 10.8% of households in Fulton County do not have access to a private motor vehicle, which is higher than the state average of 6.7%.' 'The MARTA system is a strength for those who live close to a transit station or bus stop.' Soften any 'only' wording to 'mainly'. Medium because it is a single source with 5-year-old ACS data, and Gwinnett is not covered.

## 9. Blue Ocean direction (an INFERENCE from the findings above, not itself a sourced claim). ELIMINATE: dependence on organizational e-referral networks and CBO onboarding, the feature incumbents' users barely use (8 e-referrals vs 246 shares per month). REDUCE: setup, training and enterprise integration burden, since free access plus four engagement strategies still left 7% adoption. RAISE: plain-language, patient-checked understanding of the AVS (patients flag confusion only 20% of the time), resource currency and verification (resources 'constantly fluctuate'), and help with scheduling and logistics (the factors tied to completion). CREATE: a per-step plan matched to each patient's own barriers, rated by the patient, which addresses the 56% vs 93% patient-staff gap. It should work from a paper AVS, in the patient's language, and use MARTA-aware transport options for Fulton/DeKalb.

- Confidence: low · votes: n/a (synthesis)
- Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10796276/ · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12052525/ · https://www.cbssm.org/publication_pdfs/1006/ · https://doi.org/10.1177/21501319251413968 · https://exa.ai/library/publication/hftjgdd9mx3
- Evidence: Each lever ties to a verified finding listed above. The ERRC grid itself is synthesis, and no source tests it. Incumbent pricing, who pays, and the features of Epic Emmie/MyChart AI, clearchart.ai, Unite Us and Healthify were NOT VERIFIED in this run, so the comparison against named competitors is incomplete.

## Caveats (from the run)

Many key sources are old or come from a different setting. The comprehension studies (Engel 2009, Hoek search to 2018) cover emergency department discharge, not clinic after-visit summaries, and Engel enrolled English-speaking patients only. The referral studies date from 2007 to 2018, and none is from Georgia. Applying them to ATLAS's clinic and FQHC use case is an extrapolation. The platform-adoption and perception-gap findings each rest on one site with small samples: Trenton NowPow, and Essentia's 62 patients at an 8% response rate. The Atlanta figures come from one hospital CHNA using ACS 2016-2020 data. Several parts of the research question returned NO verified evidence and must be marked NOT VERIFIED until sourced: CHW voices from Reddit or other forums, caseload sizes, time spent searching for resources, Georgia Medicaid coverage of CHW services, Grady and Mercy Care navigation programs, Gwinnett data, MARTA clinic access, and incumbent pricing, payers and complaints (findhelp, Unite Us, Healthify, Epic Emmie, clearchart.ai). The uninsured-rate claim for Fulton/DeKalb was REFUTED 0-3 and must not be used, and neither should the '38.9% never scheduled' framing (also refuted). Some verifications ran with Firecrawl out of credits, so contradiction searches were limited. The Blue Ocean grid is inference.

## Open questions (need interviews or more sourcing)

- Does Georgia Medicaid (or its CMOs: Amerigroup, CareSource, Peach State) reimburse CHW services today, and does an FQHC or Grady/Mercy Care CHW program in Atlanta have a named contact who could be a real ATLAS user?
- What do Atlanta CHWs and navigators actually use today (211, findhelp, Unite Us, paper or spreadsheets)? What is their caseload, and how many minutes does each resource search take? This needs primary interviews or forum evidence, since none was verified.
- What do findhelp, Unite Us, and patient-facing AVS explainers (Epic Emmie / MyChart AI, clearchart.ai) cost and who pays? Do any of them serve uninsured patients with paper AVS outside MyChart?
- What are current (ACS 2019-2023 or later) uninsured and LEP rates for Fulton, DeKalb and Gwinnett, given that the CHNA uninsured figures were refuted?

## Refuted: do NOT use

- (1-2) Helpers rated the platform's impact on patients higher than patients did on all 8 of the 11 experience measures where the two groups differed by 10 or more points, including the 'Simple Plan' measure. · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12052525/
- (0-3) The largest single loss point was never getting scheduled: 38.9% of referrals had no documented status. Cancellations (17.9%) and no-shows (4.0%) came after that. · https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11606-018-4392-z
- (0-3) Uninsured rates in Grady's service area exceed state and national benchmarks: 13.3% in DeKalb and 10.1% in Fulton, against 13.0% for Georgia and 8.7% for the U.S. Hispanic residents are nearly 7x (DeKalb) and nearly 6x (Fulton) more likely than White residents to be uninsured. · https://www.gradyhealth.org/wp-content/uploads/2022-Community-Health-Needs-Assessment.pdf
