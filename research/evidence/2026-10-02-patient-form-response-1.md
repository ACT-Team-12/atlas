# Patient form response 1 · 2026-10-02

- **Who (no names):** a person answering about **their own care**; Anusmita sent the form to people who regularly see doctors for chronic conditions. Age, condition and location were not asked.
- **How we reached them:** Anusmita's patient survey (Google Form), shared by her on Oct 2.
- **Interviewer:** none (written form).
- **Consent to quote (anonymous):** **yes** ("Yes, you can quote me without my name").
- **Channel:** Google Form, read by Stephen's team on Oct 2 around 1:50 pm.

## What they said (verbatim)
1. **A time it was hard to understand:** "Sometimes on the app when I try to find my results it's very difficult to even find the information and then to comprehend it since it's a lot of medical jargon"
2. **Most confusing:** "maybe the data and how to read my lab results. if there was a summary option that only highlights what I need to improve or cut back on, that'd be very helpful"
3. **What they do today:** "Ask my mom or doctor (the next time I see them). Now in the current day of AI, I would probably ask it to simplify it for me, although I've never done that before and am unsure how accurate it'd be"
4. **Talking with providers:** "Them not taking my issues seriously or brushing it off without doing any tests"
5. **Unsure about next steps:** "Honestly my own care. My doctor will give some vague advice on how to fix my problems but no actual guidance so I'm left doing it on my own"
6. **One change:** "as soon as I get my test lab results back, I would love for the doctor to call me and go through any concerning things on the list"
7. **Tried the app:** no answer.

## What it confirms or changes
- **Confirms pain point 1** (not knowing if you understood): jargon, and waiting for "the next time I see them".
- **Confirms the trust problem** our design is built on: they would ask AI but are "unsure how accurate it'd be". Every ATLAS step quotes the paper, and a second model double-checks it.
- **Confirms "vague advice, no actual guidance"**: the plan turns a paper into concrete steps.
- **New, not covered today:** lab RESULTS (values and what is out of range), not just after-visit instructions. They asked for "a summary option that only highlights what I need to improve or cut back on".

## Product change this suggests
- A results mode: paste a lab report, and ATLAS lists only the values the report itself marks out of range (its own H/L flags and reference ranges), each quoting the line, in plain words, with "ask your clinic" questions. No diagnosis and no advice beyond what the report says.

## Limits
- One person, a written form, self-selected. It is evidence about one person's experience, not a rate.
